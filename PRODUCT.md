# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Developers using Vortex to run, inspect, and steer local agent workflows on real codebases. They move between project selection, session history, approval decisions, tool output, diffs, and terminal context while trying to stay oriented and in control.

## Product Purpose
Vortex is a native desktop agent workspace for transparent local agent work. It exists to make agent behavior inspectable and governable: sessions are visible, tool activity is explicit, edits are patch-first, and risky actions stay approval-gated. Success looks like a developer being able to understand what the agent is doing, review its work quickly, and keep momentum without fighting the interface.

## Brand Personality
Thoughtful, minimal, inspectable. The product should feel calm, deliberate, and technically credible. It should read like a serious developer tool that gets out of the way of text, structure, and decision-making rather than trying to entertain or market to the user.

## Anti-references
Do not make Vortex feel like a dashboard of cards, a glossy marketing site, or a decorative "AI app" full of gradients, boxed callouts, and ornamental chrome. Avoid surfaces that compete with the content, especially in the main thread. Avoid cluttered sidebars, weak-contrast controls, and tab systems that feel secondary or unfinished.

## Design Principles
1. Keep the thread document-like: the main conversation surface is primarily text, spacing, and lightweight structure.
2. Make hierarchy do the work: use rhythm, contrast, and alignment before borders, fills, or containers.
3. Treat navigation like an IDE: project and session browsing should feel dense, calm, and dependable.
4. Make inspection first-class: diff, plan, file, and terminal views must feel intentional, not like afterthought panels.
5. Preserve trust through restraint: every visual decision should improve orientation, legibility, or control.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA contrast for standard text and controls. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Favor strong component contrast, clear focus indication, readable text-first layouts, and predictable interaction patterns suitable for long developer sessions.
